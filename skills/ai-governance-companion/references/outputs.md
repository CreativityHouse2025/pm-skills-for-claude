# Outputs

Structures for each thing this skill produces.

## AI use inventory

One row per use (not per tool).

Columns: ID; use (what it does, in a sentence); tool or system and provider; business owner; who uses it; who is affected; data categories that go in; how the output is used (assists a person, or decides); status (in use, pilot, planned, stopped); risk rating; controls in place; last reviewed.

Add a short section "Uses we suspect but have not confirmed" for things mentioned without detail, with who to ask. Do not list individuals' personal details.

## Use-case risk assessment

1. The use, the owner and the people affected
2. What could go wrong, each as a specific statement of cause, event and effect
3. Rating for each, on the organisation's scale, with the reason
4. Existing controls and their evidence
5. Additional controls proposed, with an owner for each
6. Residual rating and who accepts it
7. Decision: proceed, proceed with conditions, pause, or stop
8. Review date

For uses that significantly affect people, add an impact section: who is affected, how they are told, how they can question an outcome, and how a human can step in.

## Acceptable-use policy

Short enough that staff will read it (two to three pages for a small organisation).

1. Purpose and who it applies to
2. Which tools are approved, and how to request a new one
3. What must never go into an AI tool (by data category)
4. What AI may be used for, what needs approval, and what is not allowed
5. Checking output: who is responsible for accuracy, and when a person must review
6. Telling others: when to disclose that AI was used
7. Intellectual property and confidentiality
8. Reporting a problem or a concern
9. What happens if the policy is not followed
10. Owner, approval and review date

Write rules as plain instructions with one example each. Mark every rule the organisation has not confirmed as `SUGGESTED`.

## Roles and responsibilities

A table of activities (approve a new AI use, maintain the inventory, assess risk, sign off high-risk uses, handle incidents, train staff, review the policy) against roles, marked responsible, accountable, consulted or informed, with exactly one accountable role per activity. For small organisations, show how few people this can be.

## Supplier questionnaire

Grouped questions for a provider of an AI tool: what the system does and its limits; where data is stored and processed, and whether customer data is used to train models; security and access; accuracy, testing and known failure modes; human oversight features; transparency and documentation available; incident notification; sub-processors; certifications and audit reports held; contract terms on liability and exit. Mark which answers are essential for the organisation's intended use, and what answer would be a reason not to proceed.

## Incident process and log

**Process**: what counts as an AI incident (wrong or harmful output acted on, data entered that should not have been, unfair outcome, tool misuse, supplier failure); how to report it and to whom; first steps (stop, contain, record); who assesses severity; who must be told (internally, affected people, clients, regulators, the last to be confirmed against current legal requirements); how lessons feed back into the inventory, assessments and policy.

**Log columns**: ID; date; what happened; use affected; who and what was affected; severity; action taken; root cause; follow-up; owner; closed date.

## Gap check

Header: `Self-assessment. Not an audit, certification or legal opinion.` State the framework used and the date of the version consulted, or that the common core was used.

Table: topic; what exists; evidence; rating (in place, partly, not in place, not applicable with reason, stated not evidenced); gap; priority.

Summary: counts by rating, the three most important gaps, and what was not assessed.

## Roadmap

Three horizons: next 30 days, next quarter, later. Each item: action; which gap it closes; owner; what "done" looks like; dependency. Start with the inventory and a named owner if either is missing.

## Training and awareness plan

Audiences (all staff, people who use AI in their work, owners of AI uses, leadership); what each needs to know; format and length; frequency; how completion is recorded.

## Answering a client or regulator

Use only the governance sheet, inventory and evidence. For each question: the answer, the evidence it rests on, and any part that is `Not yet in place` stated honestly with the planned date if one is agreed. Never answer "yes" to a control that is suggested or planned.
