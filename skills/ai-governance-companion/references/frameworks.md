# Frameworks: orientation

A plain map of the main reference points and how they differ. It is general on purpose and gives no dates, deadlines, article numbers or clause text, because these change. Before stating what any of them requires, check the issuing body's current publication (see SKILL.md section 3).

## Three different kinds of thing

| | What it is | Who issues it | Binding? | Can you be certified? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ISO/IEC 42001** | A management system standard for organisations that develop, provide or use AI. It asks for policy, roles, planning, risk and impact assessment, operational controls, monitoring and continual improvement, in the same family style as other ISO management system standards. | ISO and IEC | No, voluntary, though clients may require it | Yes, by an accredited certification body |
| **NIST AI Risk Management Framework** | A voluntary method for managing AI risk, organised around four functions: Govern, Map, Measure and Manage, with companion profiles and a playbook. | US National Institute of Standards and Technology | No | No |
| **EU AI Act** | A law. It takes a risk-based approach: some practices are prohibited, some systems are treated as high risk with detailed obligations, some carry transparency duties, and the rest have few specific duties. Obligations differ by role, for example whether an organisation provides a system or deploys one. | European Union | Yes, where it applies | It has its own conformity routes; certification to a management standard is not the same thing |

A useful way to explain them: the law sets obligations, the risk framework offers a method, and the management standard gives an auditable way to run and evidence the programme. They overlap heavily, so one set of controls can serve all three, but meeting one does not automatically satisfy another.

## Other reference points

- **OECD AI Principles**: high-level international principles (such as fairness, transparency, robustness, accountability) that many national policies draw on.
- **ISO/IEC companion standards**: related standards cover topics such as AI risk management guidance and AI system impact assessment.
- **National and sector rules**: many countries and regulators (finance, health, public sector, data protection authorities) have their own AI guidance or rules. Always ask where the organisation operates and which regulators it answers to.
- **Existing obligations still apply**: data protection, consumer protection, employment, intellectual property and sector law apply to AI use whether or not there is an AI-specific law.

## Topics that almost every framework covers

Use these as the rows of a gap check when the organisation has not chosen a framework, or as a common core when it must satisfy several.

1. Leadership commitment, policy and objectives
2. Roles, responsibilities and competence
3. Inventory of AI systems and uses
4. Risk assessment and treatment
5. Impact on individuals and society
6. Data: quality, provenance, privacy, security
7. Development or procurement controls, including suppliers
8. Human oversight
9. Transparency toward users and affected people
10. Testing, validation and performance monitoring
11. Incident handling and reporting
12. Records and documentation
13. Training and awareness
14. Review, audit and improvement

## Choosing where to start

- A client or tender demands proof: a certifiable management standard is usually what they mean.
- No external driver, wants a sound internal method: a risk framework is a light way in.
- Operates in or sells into a place with an AI law: establish applicability and role first, with legal advice, then build the programme around it.
- Small and only using tools made by others: start with the common core above, scaled down.
