# Diagnostic guide

Cause areas, the signs that point to each, and what to look at to confirm. Use it to generate candidate causes, then test each against the evidence. Most troubled projects have causes in two or three areas at once.

| Area | Signs | Confirm by looking at |
|---|---|---|
| **Unclear or shifting scope** | New requests absorbed without changing date or budget; team and client describe the deliverable differently; "nearly done" for weeks | Original scope against current work; number of changes with and without approval |
| **Unrealistic plan** | Late from the first milestone; estimates made without the people doing the work; no contingency | Planned against actual duration for early tasks; who estimated; whether the plan was ever reviewed |
| **Dependencies and sequence** | Teams waiting on each other; work started before inputs were ready; rework | Which tasks slipped first and what they were waiting for |
| **Capacity and people** | Key people shared with other work; turnover; one person on the critical path | Planned against actual availability; who holds knowledge nobody else has |
| **Decisions and sponsorship** | Decisions take weeks; sponsor absent or changed; priorities conflict between leaders | Open decisions and their age; who can approve changes |
| **Requirements and quality** | Defects found late; deliverables rejected; testing squeezed | Rework volume; when users first saw the product |
| **Supplier or partner** | Late or poor deliveries; contract silent on what is going wrong; payment disputes | Contract terms against performance; who manages the supplier day to day |
| **Money** | Costs rising faster than progress; spend not tracked against work done | Budget against actual by category; committed but unpaid costs |
| **Technology or method** | New tools or approach nobody had used; integration harder than expected | What was proven before the plan was set |
| **Reporting and visibility** | Status stayed green until it suddenly was not; nobody can state percent complete with evidence | Past status reports against what was true at the time |
| **External change** | Regulation, market, client organisation or need has changed since approval | Whether the original reason for the project still holds |

## Questions that separate symptom from cause

- When was the first sign, and what changed just before it?
- If we fixed only this, would the project recover? If not, it is a symptom or a minor cause.
- Has this happened on other projects here? If so, the cause is in how the organisation works, not in this project.
- Who knew, and when? Slow bad news points to a reporting or trust problem as well as the technical one.

## Sizing the gap

Use only supplied figures and show the working.

- **Schedule:** planned finish against realistic finish, in days or weeks and as a percentage of the planned duration.
- **Cost:** planned spend to date against actual, and approved budget against forecast at completion.
- **Scope:** deliverables planned against delivered and accepted.
- **Rate check:** if a share of the work took a share of the time or budget, what does the same rate imply for the remainder? Present this as an indication, not a forecast, and say what could make it better or worse.
