# Material types

Structures for each kind of teaching material. "Learner" parts go in the handout; "Trainer" parts go in the trainer copy only.

## Case study

**Learner**
- Title and one-paragraph setting (organisation, what it does, why this project now)
- The project: purpose, what will be delivered, deadline, budget and currency
- People: names, roles, what each wants
- Situation so far: three to six dated events
- Constraints and anything already decided
- Exhibits where useful (a short email, a table of figures, a draft document)

**Trainer**
- Learning objectives the case supports
- The tension built into the case and how it tends to surface
- Deliberate gaps (information left out on purpose) and what a good learner asks
- Fact list: every name, date and amount, with calculations checked
- Ways to make it easier or harder

Length: about one page for a 30 to 60 minute session; two to three pages for a half-day workshop.

## Exercise

**Learner**
- Task in one or two sentences, starting with a verb (Build, Review, Decide, Prioritise)
- What to hand in, and in what form
- Time allowed and how to work (alone, pairs, groups of n)
- The case or the document to work on
- A blank template only if filling the structure is not itself the skill being practised

**Trainer**
- Learning objective
- Timing plan: reading, working, sharing, debrief
- Model answer (see below)
- Common mistakes to watch for while circulating
- Debrief questions (see Facilitator notes)
- Rubric, if the work is marked

Exercise formats to choose from:
- **Build**: produce a document from the case
- **Spot the mistakes**: find and fix the flaws in a given document
- **Improve**: turn a weak draft into a good one
- **Decide**: choose between options and justify it (a change request, a risk response)
- **Sequence**: put documents or steps in a sensible order and explain why
- **Role-play**: a meeting with assigned roles (sponsor, project manager, supplier) and a brief for each role

## Flawed document ("spot the mistakes")

**Learner**
- The document, looking like a real draft
- Instruction: how many problems to look for (give the number for beginners; withhold it for experienced learners)

**Trainer**
- Answer key table: number; where it is; what is wrong; why it matters; corrected version
- Which mistakes are basic and which are subtle
- Corrected full document

Plant five to eight mistakes for a 20 to 30 minute task. Draw them from the review checklist for that document. Mix types: something missing, something vague, a contradiction with the case, a wrong calculation.

## Model answer

- The completed document, built only from the case facts
- Margin notes or a short commentary: why each key choice was made
- Acceptable variations: where different answers are equally good
- What would make an answer wrong (as opposed to different)

## Grading rubric

Table with criteria down the side and three or four levels across (for example: Not yet, Developing, Proficient, Strong).

- Four to six criteria, taken from the review checklist for that document plus the learning objective
- Each cell describes what is observable in the work, not an adjective ("each risk states cause, event and effect", not "good risk statements")
- Weights per criterion if a mark is needed, totalling 100
- The pass level, if the trainer wants one
- One line on how to use it for self or peer assessment

## Facilitator notes

- Objective and where this fits in the wider course
- Materials and preparation
- Minute-by-minute timing
- How to brief the task (what to say, what not to give away)
- What to look for while learners work
- Debrief questions, moving from what happened, to why, to what they will do differently in their own work
- Likely questions from learners, with answers
- Adaptations: online delivery, larger group, less time, mixed levels

## Session pack

For "plan my session on X". Contains, in this order:
1. Session outline: objective, timing table, materials
2. Case study (learner handout)
3. Exercise sheet (learner handout)
4. Trainer copy: facilitator notes, model answer, answer key or rubric, fact list

Fit the content to the time actually available. As a guide: 30 minutes fits one short case and one focused task; 60 to 90 minutes fits a case, a task and a proper debrief; a half day fits two or three linked tasks on the same case.

## Practice questions

Only when asked. Write original scenario-based questions tied to the case or to a clear situation.

- One best answer and plausible wrong answers
- For each question: the answer, why it is best, and why each other option is weaker
- Label them as practice questions written for this course
