# Review checklists

Use the general checks on every document, then the specific block if one exists. These are prompts for judgement, not a scoring sheet: report only what matters for this project's size and stakes.

## General checks (every document)

- **Specific to this project.** Could this text be pasted into a different project unchanged? If yes, it is too generic.
- **Consistent.** Names, dates, amounts, currency and IDs match the fact sheet and other documents.
- **Measurable.** Objectives, criteria and targets have a number, a date or a clear test.
- **Owned.** Every action, risk, issue and deliverable has a named person or role.
- **Complete for its purpose.** The core sections in the catalog are present, or their absence is deliberate.
- **Honest about unknowns.** Assumptions are labelled and open questions are listed, not hidden behind confident wording.
- **Usable.** The reader it is meant for could act on it without asking the author what it means.

## Project charter (I-03)

- States why the project exists, not only what it will produce.
- Objectives have a measure and a date.
- Says what is out of scope.
- Names the sponsor and the project manager, and states what the project manager may decide alone (budget, people, changes).
- Budget and deadline are present, or their absence is explained.
- Lists the main risks and assumptions at a high level.
- Has an approval line. Without approval it is a proposal, not a charter.

## Stakeholder register (I-04)

- Includes people affected by the result, not only the team and sponsor.
- Interest and influence are assessed for each entry, not left blank or all "high".
- Each key stakeholder has a concrete engagement approach, not "keep informed" for everyone.
- Sensitive assessments are worded so the register could be seen by others without harm.

## Scope statement (P-03)

- Each deliverable has acceptance criteria someone could test.
- Exclusions are explicit; the most likely "I assumed that was included" items are named.
- Does not describe how the work will be done (that belongs in the plan).

## Work breakdown structure (P-05)

- Covers 100% of the scope statement and nothing outside it.
- Organised by deliverables, not by phases of effort or departments, unless there is a reason.
- Lowest level is a work package that can be estimated and assigned to one owner.
- Elements are nouns (things produced), not verbs (activities).
- Project management work is included.
- Every element has a unique code.

## Project schedule (P-09)

- Every work package in the WBS appears.
- Dependencies are stated; no task floats without a predecessor or successor unless it truly is independent.
- Durations have a basis (who estimated, from what).
- Milestones match the charter and fact sheet.
- The critical path is identified and the end date matches the deadline, or the gap is stated.
- Holidays, approvals and supplier lead times are accounted for.

## Cost estimate and budget (P-12)

- Costs trace to work packages or clear categories.
- Each figure has a basis of estimate.
- A contingency reserve exists and is sized to the risks, not a round guess.
- Total matches the charter and fact sheet, or the difference is explained.
- Currency and tax treatment are stated.

## Risk register (P-15)

- Each risk is written as cause, event and effect. A single word ("Delay", "Budget") is a category, not a risk.
- Probability and impact use a stated scale and are not all the same value.
- Each risk has one named owner.
- The response is an action someone will take, with a date, not a wish ("monitor closely").
- Opportunities are considered, not only threats.
- Risks already raised in the charter or fact sheet appear here.
- Has a review date; a register that is never revisited is a list, not a tool.

## Responsibility matrix (P-17)

- Exactly one Accountable per row.
- No row without a Responsible.
- Not everyone is Consulted on everything.
- Roles match the people in the fact sheet.

## Communications plan (P-18)

- Covers every key stakeholder in the register.
- States the purpose of each communication, not only its frequency.
- Each item has an owner.
- Includes how urgent issues are escalated, outside the regular rhythm.

## Status report (E-01)

- Overall status is stated up front and matches the detail below it.
- Progress is measured against milestones or baseline, not described as "going well".
- Lists decisions or help needed from the reader.
- Risks and issues shown are the current top few, with owners and dates.
- A "green" status with slipped milestones or an unresolved high issue is a contradiction: flag it.

## Change request (E-03)

- Describes the change precisely enough to estimate.
- Assesses impact on scope, schedule, cost, quality and risk, even where the answer is "none".
- Offers options, including not making the change.
- Names who decides.

## Lessons learned (E-10, C-03)

- Each lesson describes what happened and why, not only an opinion.
- Each ends in a recommendation someone can act on, with an owner.
- Includes what went well, so it is repeated.
- Avoids blaming individuals; focuses on process and conditions.

## Project closure report (C-02)

- Compares results with the charter's objectives one by one.
- Reports final scope, schedule and cost against the baseline, with reasons for variances.
- Lists open items and who now owns them.
- Confirms formal acceptance and handover.

## User story (A-03)

- Names a real user and a benefit, not a technical task.
- Small enough to finish within one sprint.
- Acceptance criteria are testable conditions.
- Does not prescribe the solution.
